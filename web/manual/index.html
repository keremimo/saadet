Saadet

One polyphonic synthesizer for the Felucca FM-1. Choose Prophet, FM6, CZ-1, JUNO-106 or HYDRA; all keyboard and accepted MIDI notes play that sound.

HYDRA

A wave-scanning engine. OSC 1 and OSC 2 each play one wave (MODE SINGLE) or SCAN a list of eight: SCAN moves through its slots, an EMPTY slot is silence. Each runs through two mutants in series: FM, STACK (five detuned copies), SYNC, PW, SQZ, WARP and HARM, each with its own WET. OSC 3 plays one wave and is a good FM source. The mixer adds ring and noise and sends a share of each source to FILTER 2. FILTER 1 has eleven types; FILTER 2 morphs from low through band or notch to high, after FILTER 1 or beside it. ENV 1 moves both filters, the scans and mutant 1; AMP ENV shapes the volume. Heavier patches play fewer notes: eight of a plain patch or of one with a mutant, six with mutants on both oscillators, fewer with heavier stacks and more mutants. From F4 up the oscillators run at twice the rate to stay clean, while the patch leaves room for it.

Eight macros, as on a Hydrasynth: each sound names its own and sends each to destinations of its choosing (scans, mutants, levels, both filters, ENV 1), each with a depth. They are HOME's knobs: K1–K4 the first four, SELECT the other four, and all eight on an SMK-37's 8 KNOBS bank. Centre is the sound as saved; turn either way. CC74 and CC71 turn macros 3 and 4, and the matrix's E1–E8 move them too. Name and route them on LFO > MACRO 1–8: NAME, the macro's VALUE, then up to three destinations, each with a DEPTH (DEST NONE frees one). A sound has 14 destinations in all. A voice is budgeted with every macro at its costliest, so no turn makes the sound late.

Besides its 56 factory waves, HYDRA has 14 user waves, U1–U14, that any slot of any sound can use: the wave lists show them after the factory waves, by their names. Make them in the editor's HYDRA panel: draw a wave, drag its harmonics, start from a factory wave, or import a .wav (one cycle, or a wavetable's frames as OSC 1's scan list). A change is heard at once in every sound that uses the wave and is kept once Saadet rests. Sounds the editor exports or adds to its library carry their user waves and bring them back to a unit's bank. Changing a user wave changes every sound that uses it.

The editor converts ASM Hydrasynth patches for HYDRA: a SysEx dump (.syx), an ASM Manager bank (.hydra) or one .patch. Import them on the HYDRA panel, into the library, or into the bank's slots. Oscillators (waves by family), mutants, mixer, filters, ENV 1, the level and the eight macros come across; ENV 2 becomes the amp envelope. LFOs, the other envelopes, most of the matrix, the arpeggiator and the effects do not, so a sound played only from the ribbon, the wheels or an LFO may be silent.

Playing

Each panel button opens a place: tap it again or turn SELECT to change its page. Hold a button to reveal its entries, then press a key to jump to one. Hold ARP instead as a sustain pedal: notes you play or let go of while it is down keep sounding after you release it, a drone the keys play over, until you press ARP again. HOME shows the sound, key and chord; K1–K4 edit its macros, and SELECT turns them to the sound's other four. On an SMK-37 the 8 KNOBS bank puts all eight on K1–K8 at once. EDIT, ENV and LFO expose the native engine controls. SCALE, CHORD, ARP and PLAY shape the performance. REC latches notes. MASTER sets the volume. OCT- and OCT+ shift the keyboard; press both to return to octave zero, or hold both for five seconds to enter update mode.

Two parts

Saadet plays one part until you ask for two. On GLOBAL > PARTS, K1 sets the mode: ONE, MULTI (MIDI channel B CH plays part B, IN CH plays part A, and the keys play the part you are on), SPLIT (notes below the SPLIT note play part B, the rest part A) or LAYER (every note plays both). With two parts on, tap HOME on HOME to change part: pages, knobs, the library and SAVE follow, and the header names the part. The parts share the voice budget. Each is guaranteed half, may borrow what the other is not using, and gives its oldest voices back when the other needs them. On the SMK-37 the PARTS pad bank picks the part, the mode and the first eight performances. A performance keeps both parts' sounds, the mode, the split, B CH and the part you were on, in one of 12 slots. Save one on SAVE's PERFORM page (turn SELECT to reach it) and load it from LIBRARY's PERFORM list.

Sounds and recall

LIBRARY (the FX button) and the editor browse factory sounds and 384 shared user slots. Native Prophet, FM6, CZ-1, JUNO-106 and HYDRA sounds share that library. Single native Prophet, DX7, CZ-1, JUNO-106 and HYDRA tones received over USB select their engine and audition immediately. Librarians can edit the sound live with native patch dumps; DX7 parameter and JUNO-106 single-control messages also edit their active engine directly. PRESETS browses the current engine; ALGORITHM changes the engine. The sound loads when the knob rests. K2 stars a sound; turn K3 SEND clockwise to export the selected sound over USB in its native SysEx format, including current native edits when that sound is playing. Sending does not load or save a sound. Hold HOME to access favourites on the keys. SAVE chooses a slot, name and favourite; press SAVE again to store. A supported SysEx dump opens SAVE, where K1 chooses its first slot and SAVE imports it. Repeated native updates retain the chosen destination. Leaving SAVE cancels the pending import while an auditioned tone keeps playing. User library slots are written only when SAVE confirms; banks wait for import and do not replace the playing sound. In J106, select FM-1 for MIDI input and output, match Saadet’s MIDI output channel, then send a tone from LIBRARY; the librarian receives individual tones rather than automatically retrieving the whole Saadet library. The complete synth state is saved automatically while idle and restored at startup. There are no projects, patterns, songs, drum kits, recording or motion lanes.

Delete and erase

For a user sound in any LIBRARY list, turn K4 DELETE up, then tap FX to confirm the slot shown in the white header. K4 back, a SOUND move, a page change, another button, or three seconds cancels. Factory sounds have no DELETE. The saved slot and its favourite are removed; the current sound keeps playing without a saved slot. There is no undo.

GLOBAL’s last page is ERASE. Turn K1 ERASE ALL up, then hold GLO for three seconds. Letting go cancels the countdown without changing page. K1 back or leaving the page cancels arming. This removes all saved user sounds, favourites, stored engine banks, settings, panel calibration and the power-on sound, then restarts with defaults. Keep power connected while progress is shown. Firmware and built-in factory sounds stay. If your FM-1 needed hardware calibration, hold OCT- and OCT+ at power-on to calibrate it again. Erasing has no undo.

MIDI and USB

Choose OMNI or one receive channel and one output channel. Scales, chords, strum, sustain, pitch bend and modulation remain available. MPC scale-pad mode uses Bank H without switching synth tracks. Tempo and MIDI clock drive the arp.

USB capture is the stereo synth master before computer playback is added, preventing monitoring feedback. Playback and capture support 16/24-bit USB formats at 44.1 kHz; internal rendering uses native 24-bit precision independently of the selected USB format.

Insert effects have been removed; effect settings in older sounds are ignored. See the panel guide for the full control map.

Editor and backups

Open the editor for Sound, FM6, Library and Settings. Settings contains complete Saadet backup and restore. Only Saadet archives are accepted; projects and Melodee backups cannot be restored.

Saadet's saved state uses flash addresses 0xA0000/0xA1000, formerly part of Melodee project A. Saving Saadet can replace that project's data. Install or update Saadet with the installer in Chrome or Edge; read the repository README before switching from Melodee. This local fork has not been validated by physical audition or control/deadline tests.
